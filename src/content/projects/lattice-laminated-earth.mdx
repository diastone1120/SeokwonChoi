## Context

Construction accounts for a large share of global carbon emissions, and conventional
materials like cement carry a heavy footprint. This project asks whether an ancient
material — earth — can be reintroduced through digital fabrication as a genuinely
low-carbon structural option.

## Material system

A lattice core is 3D-printed using FGF (Fused Granular Fabrication) from bio-based
feedstocks, then laminated with an earth-and-lime mixture reinforced with natural
fibers. Multiple fiber types were tested — coconut fiber, kenaf, rice husk, rice husk
charcoal, agar, seaweed furcata, and wheat straw — each evaluated for cost, environmental
impact, and resource availability.

## Process

Seashell waste is calcined into quicklime, hydrated into slaked lime, then combined
with earth to form the laminating mixture. The lattice geometry itself is explored
through both direct printing and non-planar toolpaths with robotic move control,
allowing the printed core to follow curved, load-adapted geometries rather than flat
layers.

## Outcomes

The work was published as a Master's thesis and presented at the 14th ISAIA 2024
Conference in Kyoto under the related paper, "Bio-Waste Wall: Advancing Sustainable
Construction with Bio-Based Materials and Manufacturing."

or tooling constraint rather than a single continuous project. Click each item for

<details class="spec-item">
<summary>Column <span class="spec-credit">AIK 2023 · Jeongseon</span></summary>
<div class="spec-body">

The print head's size limitation was tested against a 600mm origin model, capped
by a 300mm-per-print robot reach — so the column was split into four 150mm data
units suitable for printing at once. A biodegradable FGF-type formwork, printed
from seaweed-waste bioplastic, was used to cast the final piece: any digitally
designed shape can be quickly produced this way, and the formwork itself
biodegrades back into the soil once used. The final column, referencing a
traditional wooden pillar, stands 900mm tall. With Yousub Bang and Prof. Jungwon
Yoon.

<details class="spec-item">
<summary>Shellfoam <span class="spec-credit">Workshop · 2023, Seoul</span></summary>
<div class="spec-body">

Conducted under Prof. Junhee Cho, this workshop develops and applies new robot-arm
end-tips. A customized knife attached to the robot arm carves shellfoam — a
material that cuts cleanly under a blade — into parametric wave-like surfaces. The
knife's path, and the resulting piece, can be predicted and designed in advance
through Grasshopper.

</details>

<details class="spec-item">
<summary>Air <span class="spec-credit">Workshop · 2020, Seoul</span></summary>
<div class="spec-body">

Under Prof. Jie-Eun Hwang, this workshop studies air-expansion structures. A path
is drawn using a heated pharynx end-tip, and two layers of plastic vinyl are
melted together along that path to form sealed air pockets — the same principle
used to manufacture plastic bags, here repurposed to generate parametric
inflatable form. The final piece uses six vinyl layers to create five distinct air
chambers, with different-colored vinyls producing varied color and shape.

</div>
</details>

<details class="spec-item">
<summary>Chair <span class="spec-credit">Lab research · 2023, Seoul</span></summary>
<div class="spec-body">

Inspired by Frank Gehry's ribbon-chair designs, this study tests bioplastic
combinations and colors for the FGF process at product scale. Built using the
planar 3D-printing method, the chair is assembled from two separately printed
parts. Six plastic combinations were tried in total — mainly PLA and PBAT — using
the seat itself as a way to evaluate each blend's printability.

</div>
</details>

<details class="spec-item">
<summary>Concrete <span class="spec-credit">Workshop · 2018, Seoul</span></summary>
<div class="spec-body">

A school workshop producing transparent, permeable concrete by combining acrylic
sticks with UHPC. Blocks were designed with concrete experts to maintain
structural strength while maximizing UHPC's thin covering-thickness advantage —
packing in as many acrylic sticks as the pattern would allow, letting light pass
through the cast block.
